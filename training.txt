1. Calculation Logic: How Weights Vary Based on the Astronaut’s Profile

The basis is the astronaut’s body weight (W) on Earth. In microgravity, the body weight and the weight of the dumbbell are not added together; instead, the ARED machine (in the ARED/ARES app) must generate the entire load.

Step A: Base Multiplier Based on Body Weight (W)

Each exercise has its own coefficient by which the astronaut’s weight is multiplied:
• Back Squat: 1.6 × W
• Deadlift: 1.75 × W
• Heel Raises: 1.4 × W
• Bench Press: 0.85 × W
• Bent-Over Row: 0.8 × W
• Shoulder Press: 0.6 × W
• T2 Treadmill Harness Row: 0.75 × W (75% of body weight)

Step B: Adjustment Based on Gender, Age, and Morning Check-In

The app then multiplies the resulting weight from Step A by these coefficients (and rounds to the nearest 2.5 kg or 5 kg):
• Gender:
    • Male: × 1.00 (base coefficient)
    • Female: × 0.80 for lower-body exercises / × 0.70 for upper-body exercises
• Age:
    • Up to 40 years: × 1.00
	• 41–50 years: × 0.95 (slight reduction in load to protect the joints)
    • Over 50 years: × 0.90
• Bonus – Integration with the morning “Daily Check-in” (a smarter app feature):
    • If an astronaut reports poor sleep or high fatigue in the morning, the app automatically reduces the weights by 10% (× 0.90) and shortens the run to 30 minutes to prevent overtraining.
